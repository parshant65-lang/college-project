📝 CLEAN ARTICLE BODY:

India’s wait for its first gold from the Aichi-Nagoya Asian Games continued on the second day even as the shooters added three more medals to the tally, including a double podium finish in the 10m air rifle, in Nagoya on Monday (September 21, 2026).

Himanshu Dhillon proved he was ready for the big stage, winning his maiden international individual medal at the senior level, pipping Rudrankksh Patil to a silver in a nail-biting, dramatic final that saw defending champion Sheng Lihao retain his title. The two, together with Parth Mane, also won silver as a team. India had won gold in the team event last time around, with Rudrankksh the only constant.

Himanshu, a week away from his 21st birthday, played a steady hand all through, staying in the top three at all times with no signs of being a debutant in the spotlight, even as senior pro Rudrankksh and Sheng kept exchanging the lead at the top. Till the 15th shot, in fact, Rudrankksh was comfortably in the lead before Sheng crawled back even as Himanshu consolidated his spot.

Shooter Himanshu Dhillon does the star turn as India wins two silver and as many bronze medals on the second day of the Aichi-Nagoya Asian Games.📹The Hindu pic.twitter.com/oy68zLUopq

By the 17th shot, the potential medallists had been clearly identified, with only the colour of the metal to be decided. A 9.9 on the 18th by Himanshu sent the sizeable Indian presence in the stands into a tense silence before the youngster fought back with a couple of 10.5s.

It was on the 21st shot that the equations turned. Himanshu managed an impressive 10.6 while Rudrankksh, in second position, could only manage a 9.7. It was the lowest score he had shot the whole day, and it came at the worst possible time. It helped Himanshu leapfrog into second spot, and even though the former found a 10.6 on his final shot, he fell agonisingly short by 0.1 points to finish third.

It was clear at that point that Sheng would walk away with the gold — he had built a two-point difference with two shots to go over Himanshu. But to the youngster’s credit, he didn’t give up. His final two shots were 10.8 and 10.9, as close to perfection as possible, but Sheng matched up for a new Games and Asian record of 254.2 points, bettering his own previously set mark of 253.3.

Earlier in the qualification, the Indian trio combined for a total of 1890.1 points while Sheng, along with Ma Sihan and Zhang Changhog, totalled 1899 for China for a new World record, shadowing India’s 1893 three years earlier.
