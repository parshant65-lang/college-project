📝 CLEAN ARTICLE BODY:

Minister for School Education Rajmohan on Sunday said the cultural pride of Tamils lay not only in their antiquity but also in continuation of their ancient art, culture and lifestyle traditions to the present day.

He was addressing an event organised to release books and research papers as part of Indus Valley Day held at Porunai museum here.

Describing the event as important, he touched upon the history of Tamil Nadu, particularly the significance of Porunai civilisation. Every artefact unearthed through archaeological studies highlighted an important chapter of history. The modern technological facilities set up at the museum, including 5D film and VR technology, were designed to make visitors experience history rather than merely view it.

The archaeological evidence demonstrated the scientific progress of the Tamils. Each artefact, including pottery, metal objects and inscriptions, helped to understand the lifestyle of a particular period. “Very high temperatures are required for iron technology. Archaeological evidence is being found that the Tamils used such technology in ancient times. The items found in excavations, including Keezhadi, especially a Tamil inscription dating back to about 2,500 years, are important evidence that the Tamils were a literate society,” he said.

Steps would be taken to establish a souvenir shop, research centre, restaurant and other facilities at the museum. Researchers and students could study and conduct research in a peaceful environment at the centre, he added.

Director of Archaeology V.P. Jeyaseelan, Collector Anand Mohan and other officials were present.
