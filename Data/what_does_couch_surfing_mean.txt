📝 CLEAN ARTICLE BODY:

“I don’t know why Vrinda agreed to marry Ganpat. He’s not very good looking and he…”

“True, he’s not great looking. But he’s a wonderful person. Very kind. You know what they say, ‘handsome is as handsome does’.”

“It’s a roundabout way of saying good looks don’t really matter. An individual’s worth is determined by how he is with others – how he treats others.”

“In other words, judge someone by his actions – what he does, and how he treats people. Don’t go merely by looks. Looks can be deceiving.”

“Very good! Whenever my grandmother caught my brother standing in front of the mirror for a long time, she used to say, handsome is as handsome does.”

“My boss is very good looking, but he’s mean to all those who work under him. Handsome is as handsome does, I guess.”

“One can also say ‘pretty is as pretty does’! Priya might not be much to look at, but she’s a very helpful person. You know what they say, pretty is as pretty does.”

“That’s true. Sheba tells me that Rakesh is taking a couple of months off.”

“Yes, he’s going to be doing some amount of travelling. He’s spending a couple of months in Thailand and Vietnam. He says it’s been his life-long dream.”

“Really? Either he has a lot of money or he knows a lot of people in those countries.”

“Couch-surfing? Never heard that term before. What does it mean?”

“The term was originally used for international travelling. When you do ‘couch-surfing’, you stay in a stranger’s home for free.”

“But you don’t get the normal convenience or comfort of a hotel. You may not even have a room of your own. You may be asked to sleep on a couch or the floor.”

“Which explains why it’s called ‘couch-surfing’, I guess.”

“Exactly! Although you get to stay free of charge, you might be expected to help with the household chores. That’s what most guests do.”

“Families which allow you to couch-surf advertise online. Rakesh’s brother did a month of couch-surfing in Brazil last year.”

“A family allowed him to stay with them for a month? That was…”

“When you couch-surf, you don’t stay in a place for too long. You keep moving from one place to another. Couch-surfing is something you probably do when you’re young.”

“That’s true. How about this example? I’m planning to visit Sri Lanka in December. Maybe, I should couch-surf and see what the experience is like.”

“Nowadays, the term has begun to be used when you’re travelling within your own country and staying with people you may or may not know.”

“Your friend’s place or a relative’s. Mohan and I going are going to be spending a couple of weeks in Mumbai looking for a job. We’ll be couch-surfing at the homes of our former classmates.”

“Our flat wasn’t ready. We were forced to couch-surf in several houses for over three weeks.”
